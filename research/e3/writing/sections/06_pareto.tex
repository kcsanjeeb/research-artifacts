\section{The Corrected Pareto Accounting}
\label{sec:pareto}

\subsection{The frame-rate correction}
An autopsy of our own sweep found the npy video path double-resampled to an effective 0.25 fps against 0.5-fps anchors. Corrected at true 0.5 fps (standing assertion now live): the multi-grain store's accuracy rises 51.1 $\to$ \textbf{52.4} ($+1.3$, $p{=}0.093$ — the extra frames genuinely help), and \textbf{store bytes double to 10.38 GB/h} (fixed per-segment token budgets times twice the segments). The pre-fix storage ratio had quietly compared our half-rate store against competitors' full-rate numbers; we retract it.

After multiplicity correction over the full 21-comparison family: \textbf{no accuracy claim survives} — the honest statement is that \sys matches CVPR'26 SOTA accuracy and is statistically indistinguishable from full-fidelity ReKV. The storage position is likewise honest: $8.4$--$11.4\times$ MuKV's published storage in fp16; the int4-group-scale repair (answer-level verified: naive int4 flips 4/5 greedy answers; group scales pending measurement) is the only route to parity, and we report its outcome separately rather than assuming $4\times$.

\subsection{The pre-registered Pareto attempt}
Whether a low-tax operating point (corrected $\approx$5.6 GB/h) plus verified int4 group scales reaches storage parity at matched accuracy is an engineering question with a pre-registered gate; its outcome strengthens or closes this section but does not change the paper. Whatever it yields, the claim ceiling is fixed in advance: \emph{matches SOTA accuracy at comparable storage with a different architecture}. We do not write ``beats'' for any margin that fails Holm — in any document, at any point.

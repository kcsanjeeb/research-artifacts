\section{Six Mechanisms, Six Post-Mortems}
\label{sec:mech}

Each mechanism below was proposed from the literature's own admitted openings, built, trap-verified, and killed by a pre-registered gate. Full run directories (config, environment, code state, metrics, judged artifacts) accompany each.

\subsection{M1--M3: the inside-the-model trio (all dead)}
\textbf{Differential KV coding} (residuals against keyframes, codec-style) died at its gate: pre-RoPE key residuals are 0.25--0.46 of block energy (values 0.54--0.94), because stored KV is a function of the whole prefix — adjacent-frame difference measures rotary position drift, not content change (differencing post-RoPE keys would inflate residuals $73\times$). Compression at matched reconstruction error reached $1.62$\,GB/h — $0.56\times$ of MuKV's published rate, where the gate needed $3\times$, and $\sim$1.0$\times$ over trivially quantizing every frame independently. \textbf{Adaptive GOP sizing} died with it: threshold-triggered GOP length ratios across static/dynamic regimes were 0.74--1.11 at every threshold — fixed-stride keyframing in disguise, with no event-boundary alignment. \textbf{Retrieval-adaptive precision} died on two measurements: 2-bit quantization distortion is \emph{flat} across selection-frequency quintiles (Hot/Cold ratio 0.99, gate 1.5), and 4-bit is near-lossless at logit level everywhere — with no sensitivity gradient, adaptive bit-width has nothing to allocate (see TB-findings in Appendix~\ref{app:extra} for the answer-level int4 caveat).

\subsection{M4--M5: position disentanglement and write-time debiasing}
\textbf{Position-disentangled store} (pre-RoPE content keys + position sidecar) was built and survived trap-verification, with one correction: exact-position fetch at true streaming positions ($\sim$86k tokens) collapses generation — answers truncate mid-sentence late-stream, the signature of rotary out-of-distribution. Rank-remapped fetch restores bit-exact stock behavior (10/10 character-identical at the failure scale; benchmark delta 0.0). The literature's re-indexing patchwork~\cite{livevlm,streammem,hermes} reads, in hindsight, as partial fixes to this same fetch-position problem. \textbf{Write-time debiasing} measurably works at the statistic level (attention-vs-position residual 0.412 $\to$ 0.330) and measurably does nothing at the answer level (paired $p{=}0.077$): MuKV's positional bias is real, and removing it does not move RVS-Ego accuracy.

\subsection{M6: deferred query-time commitment (the ambitious one)}
The flagship idea — store a hedged superset at write time, commit grains per query with cross-grain consistency and entropy-triggered refinement — failed at every valid configuration: deferred beat write-time commitment by $+0.8$ (tax 0.5) and $+1.6$ (tax 0.75, later shown degenerate-by-quantization), both paired-$p \approx 0.3$ under a pre-declared gate of $+2$. The entropy second pass is inert (fires 10.8\%, zero gain where it fires). The hedged superset has no operating point below tax 0.5 (a per-signal frame-quantization dead zone, kept visible as a known limitation). The system's single-grain ablations do validate one design choice: no single grain suffices (patch-only 45.6 / frame-only 43.4 / segment-only 41.1 vs the multi-grain system $\sim$52).

\subsection{The seventh candidate that never ran}
A learned commitment scorer was the planned successor to M6. A pre-registered learnability probe — can a trivial selector predict the winning grain policy from question metadata and store statistics? — returned $\sim$0 held-out gain (200 seeded resplits: mean $-1.02\pm1.75$ pt; 26\% of splits positive), and the oracle analysis (Section~\ref{sec:null}) shows the headroom it would harvest is an artifact. The mechanism was abandoned \emph{before} implementation, on evidence — the cheapest possible kill.
